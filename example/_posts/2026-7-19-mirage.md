Got aboard our ship in the morning, heading to Melina. James and Jasper decided to stay behind in the city, since it suited them and their needs. We gave them a bit of food and some of our coffers so they could travel comfortably should they need, and we parted on amicable terms. I hope we run into them again, but James seemed relieved to have found a relatively safe place to settle for a little while.

The boat we set sail on was nice, but the captain was a bastard. he an outright unpleasant fellow who didn't make smalltalk other than to be a putz, and the events of the day didn't exactly warm me to him.

I was glad to have a crewed vessel though, and to not be the one in charge of figuring out how to steer a boat. I haven't had to channel Tazam's knowledge for a week or two now, and I'm grateful for that. The yellow faction has been more than willing to lend their skills to our cause, and I'm honestly starting to warm to them.

Our journey to Melina was, unfortunately, a rather eventful one. The captain warned us of these strange green bluffs we were heading towards, and before we knew it we were thrown into an odd state.

Our bodies collapsed, and it seems our spirits were rudely thrust out of them. Time stood still as we watched our own bodies, it was a very unnerving experience. I spoke to the captain, but he could not hear me, he couldn't even move. In our spirit forms, we saw the lights of this land's gods and how they were affecting us, connecting us to the dieties who had chosen us.

And then, we were seperated and isolated, away from one another. Everyone began to see visions, people from their past who spoke with them.

It sounded like Luisa and Connor both met with their respective parents, and had pretty different reactions to doing so. Grem wouldn't speak of what she saw, but she seemed highly disturbed by whatever it was. 

I saw Katherine again. She tapped me on the shoulder, gave me that half-grin, and I melted. I'd never been more relieved in my life; to see her in that darkness... to remember her face and her smile... to embrace... It was wonderful. I was lost for a moment, without sense or measure, just relieved to have found her at last.

But, that was not Katherine. I don't think I knew it right away, but something got through to me and it clicked. No, that Katherine was dressed how I remember... smiling, like how I remember. Why would you wear that old outfit, why would your bandages still be fresh? 

Would you really smile like that, when you saw me again? I pray you would, but... the last smile you gave me was so very different from those we shared before. I'm inclined to believe that was the most truthful of the bunch, something sweet amidst a bitter situation.

I do hope though, if I should die before we reunite, that you take me at my word. Even though it was but a mirage, that was one of the happiest moments of my life. I pray it comes to reality, but if not... know that I am happy to have come this far in pursuit of you, and I have no regrets about chasing after that glimpse of happiness. There is nothing that I treasure more, and nothing I would not do for a chance at that life for us.

Unfortunately, I feel I came out one of the few victors in the spat with the hallucinations. The rest of the crew was rather distraught after the event, and their spirits could not be raised. The captain had evidentally found his own way of dealing with the visions, and taunted us for falling victim to it. Noggin was the one who really got us all out of our stupors, and seemed the least affected of our lot.

We made short time to Melina afterwards, and before we departed onto land, Connor found a fragment of the bluffs that had fallen onto the ship's deck. We took it for use later, since it seemed magical in nature. It seems to allow for us to force a similar event to occur with the parting of spirit and body. We had thought it may be useful in curing Grem's curse, or perhaps at least alleviating it, but Luisa's paladin magics managed to rid Grem of the condition before we could test out the theory. Grem also revealed to us, when we made land, that her patron was none other than Kytheo himself, a... troubling revelation to say the least. She claimed that Kytheo had been injured in a fight recently, and knew little of what to expect on our confrontation of the man.

The ship and captain promised to wait until our return, so we made for the Fomorian city in preparation for confronting Kytheo.

The city was not particularily welcoming, we stuck out like a sore thumb in its midst. It was clear that all manner of fomor made this place their home, and had little love for the races that occupied the mainland. At first, it was mostly just stares and whispers upon our arrival, but many of the vendors flat out refused to sell us goods by the time we reached the marketplace.

We spent time talking with a witch though, who told us a bit of her faith and what to expect when scaling the island's volcano. She recommended us offerings to bring to the "moon gods" (deathcarrots and ceremonial meats. She did not know if the carrots were fit for human consumption). As aforementioned, though, we did not find vendors willing to sell us such goods. It was only with the witch's help that we managed to acquire those offerings. We paid her well for them, and I bought Luisa a small trinket from the witch's wares as a thank you.

I do not know how strong of a connection these people have to the orcs I've come to know, but they spoke almost exclusively orcish and showed a similar disdain for the other races. This is the second orcish religion I've come across where the other races are seen are evil or inferior, and I hate to think it a trend, but travel through this area was difficult. Tonoway always treated me well, but he spoke poorly of humans among the Gruumsh scripture. I won't say it was what caused me to leave that tribe, but it was certainly a reason for why I never thought to stay.

Regardless, we prepared our offerings and intended to set out towards the volcano. Unfortunately, we found that a group of young pickpockets had stolen Grem's bell, and one had cursed themselves by mistake. We rushed back to help the youth. Luisa had figured out a means to lift the curse on Grem on the boat ride over, and was able to save the child as well. The children were frightened though, and lashed out at us even as we tried to help. We managed to make it away without a fight, but not without a physical altercation. I'm loathe to return to that city, both out of shame for the incident and fear of how frighting we must have been to these kids. I'm proud of Luisa for her actions though, she was able to save both Grem and this child where I could not, and she came to my aid when I was kicked to the ground.

We left the city quickly, and left a small portion of our offering at the shrines leading up the volcano. The journey up was thankfully rather uneventful, though Connor and I did not fare particularily well on the journey. The heat was dibilitating, and I doubted my ability to reach the top unaided, but Luisa was able to summon a steed to help aid us (it was a rather terrifying creature, but it took well to the deathcarrots we had for the gods, and was very amicable afterwards. We even rode it across the lava itself at the top of the mountain, and it seemed to be both a reliable and friendly companion. I prefer it to Bingus, though it is no slight against the mole).

At the top of the mountain was a simple shack, and within the shack an impossibly large and ornate space. We were welcomed in, expected by a god named Belvedere. He was a strange fellow, very tall and elf-life, but with gigantic talons on his hands and feet and skin so black it absorbed light. He bade us remove our shoes and follow him in to speak with Kytheo, and accepted our offerings though spoke to them being misguided. Once he dropped us off with Kytheo, he asked us not to make a mess, then took his leave.

We complied and met with Kytheo, who assumed our reasoning for being there. We expected Grem to handle the negotiations with her patron, but she seemed less than inclined to talk with the man. Instead, Noggin, Luisa, and I took Kytheo up on his old offer to trade, back when we had first run into him as Halfdane. He seemed delighted to barter with us, and [a few words here have been erased] he asked us to inspect the blade we had come for, before negotiations began.

None of us could pull the sword from the sheathe, but Kytheo reassured us it would respond to the owner upon a fair deal. When the blade was in my hands, I inspected it with magic, and found that it was likely exactly what we were looking for, and... that Kytheo was likely being straightforward with us on this matter. We offered him Luisa's black shard knife in exchange, the opposite blade to his own, and he accepted the trade without question. Luisa obtained Kytheo's blade, and Kytheo returned the black shard to his brother, Belvedere, the hound. Whatever bad blood the two shared, some of it must have been alleviated through that gesture.

Luisa practiced with the blade, and found it to be a very powerful artifact. I offered my own hand to trial, and with but a pinprick of the blade upon my wrist, I lost all feeling in the hand, despite no physical damage. Connor was able to restore my hand to its original state by using the stone from the cliffs to enter into a spirit world and reconnect the severed hand with my arm. But, I find it to be a strange fix. My hand does not move quite as well as it once did, like there's still a remnant of damage through the action. It's not dissimilar to nerve damage, sans the pain. It responds just a hair too slow to my commands, but it does not hurt, and it does not look damaged.

Perhaps more impressively though, Luisa is able to cut holes through space, allowing us to travel to locations we have been to before. She managed to open a door to the place where our ship sat on the shore, and another to her own homeland. We mused over the idea of sending some people home using this blade, but... Kytheo warned us that should we leave before the work was done on this realm, it would come back to bite us.

We left Kytheo at that, as he had no other asks of us and no assignments other than to return once our work had been done. He personally offered to return us home afterwards, and we offered to try to return the blade at the end of all of this. After thanking him for his trade, Belvedere escorted us out. Before we left him, Belvedere did ask Connor to deliver a message of vengence from him to the gods we were to slay. It seems he is a hound waiting for some sort of master, one that Kytheo does not think will return. They seem to be at odds on this matter though, and Belvedere seems to be the more... direct of the two when it comes to matters. He insinuated that Kytheo acted more through proxys, and given Grem's... choices... I think I understand the meaning in that. It does make me curious as to what Belvedere may be up to, though.

He also seemed to be fond of the ceremonial meat we dropped off, though he was quick to hide that at first. We should bring him a nicer gift of it next time.

On our way out, Grem and Kytheo had a word in private. I know not what it was about.

Soon after, we took our leave and boarded the boat.
